## [en] Clothing, occasion and local variation

At a Lhosar the dressing begins long before anyone reaches the ground. In a back room a skirt is shaken out and stepped into, a blouse is buttoned to the throat, a length of cloth is wound twice at the waist and pinned, another is folded over one shoulder, and the head cloth goes on last, when the woman is already standing and cannot sit down again without undoing the work. Then she walks out into the cold, and from thirty metres away the outfit is unmistakable.

Unmistakable, and yet not a uniform. There is no single set of clothes that every Tamu wears, and the sources make that plain in a way worth noticing. The older guide records substantial change in fabrics and in everyday clothing over the period it covers, describing what people wore in the villages its author knew. The Los Angeles Gurung (Tamu) Society presents a community description of ceremonial dress, written by and for people who wear it now, on occasions they choose. The two have different purposes and different periods, and neither is wrong.

That gap is the honest starting point for this chapter. A description written decades ago fixes a moment; a community account fixes a use. Read together, they show a tradition that has been continuously adjusted — cloth bought instead of woven, a style simplified for a hall in California, a grandmother's ornaments brought out of a box for a wedding. Local names differ, colours differ, the cloth available differs, and so does what a particular family prefers. A present-day outfit should therefore not be treated as an exact replica of one historical description, nor that description as a rule the present has broken.

What follows is a description by function and occasion rather than a fixed glossary: what a woman puts on, in what order, and why the order matters.

Two photographs taken thirty years apart in the same village usually show the same silhouette and different cloth, and that is the most useful thing an old album can teach: the form is older than the fabric, and the fabric dates the picture.

What the two accounts share is a set of functions — covering, carrying, signalling an occasion — and it is through the functions rather than the fabrics that the tradition can be followed across a century.

## [ne] पोसाक, अवसर र स्थानीय भिन्नता

ल्होसारमा, विवाहको तस्बिरमा वा मञ्चमा गुरुङ पोसाक तुरुन्तै चिनिन्छ — तर सबै तामुले लगाउने एउटै पोसाक छैन। पुरानो मार्गदर्शिकाले कपडा र दैनिक पहिरनमा ठूलो परिवर्तन अभिलेख गर्छ, जबकि लस एन्जलस गुरुङ (तामु) सोसाइटीले कर्मकाण्डी पोसाकको सामुदायिक विवरण दिन्छ, र ती दुई स्रोतका प्रयोजन र कालखण्ड फरक छन्। स्थानीय नाम, रङ, उपलब्ध कपडा र पारिवारिक रुचि फरक पर्छ, त्यसैले आजको पोसाकलाई कुनै ऐतिहासिक विवरणको ठ्याक्कै प्रतिलिपि मान्नु हुँदैन।

पहिले महिलाले वास्तवमा के लगाउँछिन् भन्नेबाट सुरु गरौँ।

## [en] Women’s garments and jewellery

A full outfit is built in layers, and the layers go on in sequence: a skirt, a high-necked velvet blouse, a waist wrapping, a velvet cloth over the shoulder and a head cloth. Put on in that order, the layers are also a sequence of constraint and display. The skirt and the waist wrap hold the body and set its line; the blouse and the shoulder cloth carry the colour; the head cloth finishes the silhouette and frames the face. Remove any one and the rest look unfinished, which is why a woman dressing in a hurry still gets the order right.

The guide distinguishes a wider pleated traditional skirt from cloth bought and used as a lungi, a distinction that records an economy as much as a style: the pleated skirt is the older form, the purchased cloth the cheaper and later one, and both appear in the same villages in the same decade. The same source mentions gold ornaments and turquoise or other semi-precious necklaces, and connects them with trade and town markets rather than with anything made in the village — the stones arrived along the routes that carried salt, grain and, later, soldiers' pay.

Garment names need care. Terms such as ghalek, or ghalek-type words, should not be assumed to mean the blouse in every account, because in some descriptions they refer to a shoulder wrapping instead. A reader who takes one glossary as universal will misread half the photographs. Where terminology needs local verification, this chapter identifies clothing by its function — the skirt that is pleated, the cloth that crosses the chest, the wrap that holds the waist — rather than pretending to a fixed vocabulary that the sources do not support.

## [ne] महिलाका पोसाक र गहना

पूरा पोसाक तह-तहमा बन्छ: स्कर्ट, अग्लो घाँटीको भेल्भेट ब्लाउज, कम्मरमा बेर्ने कपडा, काँधमा भेल्भेटको कपडा र शिरको कपडा। मार्गदर्शिकाले फराकिलो प्लेटेड परम्परागत स्कर्टलाई किनेको लुङ्गी कपडाबाट छुट्याउँछ, र व्यापार तथा बजारसँग जोडिएका सुनका गहना र टर्कीज वा अरू अर्ध-बहुमूल्य हारको उल्लेख गर्छ। पोसाकका नाममा होसियारी चाहिन्छ: घलेक वा घलेक-जातिका शब्दलाई हरेक विवरणमा ब्लाउज भनेर नबुझ्नुहोस्, ती काँधमा बेर्ने कपडालाई पनि जनाउन सक्छन्। जहाँ शब्दावलीमा स्थानीय पुष्टि चाहिन्छ, यो अध्यायले पोसाकलाई त्यसको कामअनुसार चिनाउँछ।

## [en] The bhangra and men’s clothing

The bhangra is the carrying cloth, or back-sack arrangement, that crosses the chest and hangs open at the back. It is the piece of dress that surprises visitors most, because it looks ceremonial and is in fact practical, and the two are not in conflict.

Older descriptions connect carrying garments with woven nettle fibre, worn alongside a waist wrap, a shirt and a Nepali cap; contemporary ceremonial bhangra may use white purchased fabric instead of nettle, which changes the material and leaves the shape alone. The shape is the point. A man crossing a valley with a load of grain, or with a child on his back, needed a cloth that would hold the weight and release it quickly at the other end, and a garment built for that has a reason to survive into festivals where nothing is carried at all.

Its practical role therefore matters to how the object is read. A familiar festival form grew out of carrying needs, not simply decoration, and the same caution applies to the accessories. A khukuri or a cap in an outfit is not proof of military service, and it is not an object every Gurung man must wear; both are elements a family may include or leave out, and a photograph that shows them tells you about a choice, not about a rule. The safest reading of any single item is to ask what it did before it became ornamental.

## [ne] भाङ्ग्रा र पुरुषको पोसाक

भाङ्ग्रा भनेको छाती पार गरेर पछाडि खुला रहने बोक्ने कपडा वा पिठ्युँको झोलाको व्यवस्था हो। पुराना विवरणले बोक्ने पोसाकलाई बुनिएको सिस्नोको रेसासँग जोड्छन्, सँगै कम्मरको बेर्ना, सर्ट र नेपाली टोपी। आजको कर्मकाण्डी भाङ्ग्रा सेतो किनेको कपडाको पनि हुन सक्छ। यसको व्यावहारिक भूमिका महत्त्वपूर्ण छ: परिचित चाडको रूप सजावट मात्र होइन, बोक्ने आवश्यकताबाट बन्यो। पोसाकमा देखिने खुकुरी वा टोपी सैनिक सेवाको प्रमाण होइन, न हरेक गुरुङ पुरुषले लगाउनैपर्ने वस्तु हो।

## [en] Materials tell another history

Look closely at an old garment and the material tells a second history, one that the shape conceals. Harrison and Macfarlane document backstrap weaving and the processing of nettle fibre — the plant cut, retted, stripped, spun and woven at home — while the guide discusses local wool and, later, imported cotton and wool arriving through the bazaars.

Some household textiles were woven at home; others were bought. Cotton cloth, imported wool and ready-made garments came from bazaars and later from shops, and a single outfit may combine both in a way that no photograph can sort out: a home-woven skirt with a blouse of imported velvet, nettle fibre next to chemical dye, a machine-stitched seam inside a hand-pleated panel. As materials changed, familiar forms stayed meaningful, which is how clothing survives an economy — the shape absorbs the new cloth and keeps its name.

This is also where a reader should slow down. Without examining the fibre or knowing who made the garment, a photograph cannot establish whether an item is handwoven nettle, wool, cotton or a modern substitute. Catalogues that caption such images as "traditional dress" are describing a look rather than a material, and the material is where the labour, the trade and the date actually live.

## [ne] सामग्रीले अर्को इतिहास बताउँछ

पुरानो पोसाक नजिकबाट हेर्नुहोस्, कपडाले दोस्रो इतिहास भन्छ। Harrison र Macfarlane ले पिठ्युँको तानमा बुनाइ र सिस्नोको रेसा प्रशोधन अभिलेख गर्छन्, जबकि मार्गदर्शिकाले स्थानीय ऊन र पछि आयातित कपास तथा ऊनको चर्चा गर्छ। केही घरेलु कपडा घरमै बुनिन्थ्यो; अरू — कपास, आयातित ऊन, तयार पोसाक — बजार र पछि पसलबाट किनिन्थ्यो। कपडा बदलिँदै जाँदा परिचित रूपहरू अर्थपूर्ण रहे। रेसा नहेरी वा बुन्नेलाई नचिनी तस्बिरले वस्तु हातले बुनेको सिस्नो, ऊन, कपास वा आधुनिक विकल्प हो भनी स्थापित गर्न सक्दैन।

## [en] Dress as a living choice

The older accounts include young people wearing changing styles and women combining different garments, and that detail should not be reduced to a story in which modern clothing destroys identity. A girl in a school uniform and a grandmother in a full ceremonial set are both wearing what their lives require, and in many households both are the same family on the same day.

Ceremonial dress can communicate belonging even when everyday life uses other clothes entirely. A man who has not worn a bhangra since childhood will wear one for his father's funeral; a woman who left the village at eighteen will borrow a set for her daughter's wedding. The outfit is not a measure of how traditional a person is. It is a statement made at a particular time, to a particular audience, and the audience usually knows exactly what it means.

Inherited jewellery carries more than appearance. A grandmother's necklace is an heirloom before it is an ornament, and its value in a family's memory may have nothing to do with its price. That is why any detailed catalogue would need a named owner or maker, a region, a date, the garment term used by the family and permission to publish — rather than treating one photograph, taken at one event, as a national standard. The caution is not academic housekeeping. A photograph without those details turns a person's heirloom into an illustration.

## [ne] पोसाक जीवित छनोटका रूपमा

पुराना विवरणमा युवाले बदलिँदो शैली लगाएको र महिलाले फरक पोसाक मिलाएको देखिन्छ, जसलाई आधुनिक पोसाकले पहिचान नष्ट गर्छ भन्ने कथामा खुम्च्याउनु हुँदैन। दैनिक जीवनमा अरू कपडा भए पनि कर्मकाण्डी पोसाकले अपनत्व बोल्न सक्छ, र पुर्ख्यौली गहनाले देखिने रूपभन्दा बढी पारिवारिक सम्झना बोक्न सक्छ। विस्तृत सूचीकरणका लागि नाम खुलेको धनी वा बुन्ने, क्षेत्र, मिति, पोसाकको शब्द र अनुमति चाहिन्छ, एउटै तस्बिरलाई राष्ट्रिय मानक मान्नु हुँदैन।

## [en] Cloth, wealth and the loom

Dress was also an economic system, and the loom was where it started. Women wove on backstrap looms, turning nettle fibre, sheep's wool and imported cotton into cloth for household use and for exchange, so a woman's working hours produced objects that could be worn, given or sold.

The ornaments worn at festivals — coral, turquoise, gold and silver — represented family wealth in a form that could be displayed, inherited and, in hard times, sold. A woman's jewellery was often her own property, distinct from her husband's land, and could serve as savings or insurance: something to be pledged when a son needed passage money, or released when a harvest failed.

That is a significant fact about power inside a household, and it is easy to walk past. Wealth a woman could carry, wear and dispose of was wealth she controlled, in a society where land usually passed through men. When men left for military service, the ornaments they sent home became visible evidence of remittance income, so the style and quantity of jewellery in a village photograph records migration as well as taste. Reading an old photograph well means asking not only what the women are wearing but who paid for it.

Cloth was also the product of a household's own labour time, which made a finished garment a different kind of property from grain: it could be worn, given at a wedding, pledged for a loan or sold in a bad year without anyone's permission.

## [ne] कपडा, सम्पत्ति र तान

पोसाक आर्थिक व्यवस्था पनि थियो। महिलाले पिठ्युँको तानमा सिस्नोको रेसा, भेडाको ऊन र आयातित कपास घरायसी प्रयोग तथा विनिमयका लागि कपडामा बुन्थे। चाडमा लगाइने गहना — मूँगा, टर्कीज, सुन र चाँदी — देखाउन, उत्तराधिकारमा सार्न र अप्ठ्यारोमा बेच्न सकिने रूपमा पारिवारिक सम्पत्ति थियो। महिलाको गहना प्रायः उनकै सम्पत्ति हुन्थ्यो, पतिको जग्गाबाट अलग, र बचत वा बीमाको काम गर्न सक्थ्यो। पुरुष सैनिक सेवामा जाँदा घर पठाइएका गहना रेमिट्यान्स आयको देखिने प्रमाण बन्थे, त्यसैले गाउँको तस्बिरमा गहनाको शैली र मात्रा स्वादजत्तिकै आवागमनको अभिलेख हो।

## [en] Dressing abroad

In the diaspora, traditional dress has become both rarer and more deliberate. Few families own a full set of everyday village clothing, and the elaborate ornaments are usually reserved for weddings and Lhosar, kept in a box for the rest of the year and insured, in some households, more carefully than anything else in the house.

Second-generation children may wear a simplified ghalek or a waistcoat for a school cultural day, and community organisations keep sets of costumes to lend for performances, which means a child in Britain may wear a garment whose name they do not know, borrowed from a cupboard in a community hall, for an audience of teachers. Dress is no longer what people wear but what they choose to wear to show belonging, and the choosing makes it more visible and more self-conscious at the same time.

That raises practical questions the community discusses openly: authenticity, cost, and the risk of turning a living tradition into a costume. A rented outfit for a school assembly is not the same thing as a grandmother's wedding set, and treating them as equivalent flattens both. The people who conduct the rites for which this dress is worn are the subject of the next chapter.

## [ne] विदेशमा पोसाक

प्रवासमा परम्परागत पोसाक विरलै र जानाजान लगाइने भएको छ। थोरै परिवारसँग दैनिक गाउँले पोसाकको पूरा सेट हुन्छ, र विस्तृत गहना प्रायः विवाह र ल्होसारका लागि राखिन्छ। दोस्रो पुस्ताका बालबालिकाले विद्यालयको सांस्कृतिक दिनमा सरल घलेक वा वास्कट लगाउन सक्छन्, र सामुदायिक संस्थाले प्रदर्शनका लागि पोसाकको सेट सापटी दिन्छन्। पोसाक अब मानिसले लगाउने कुरा होइन, अपनत्व देखाउन छान्ने कुरा बन्यो, जसले यसलाई बढी देखिने र बढी आत्म-सचेत बनाउँछ, र प्रामाणिकता, खर्च तथा जीवित परम्परालाई पोसाकमा बदल्ने जोखिमबारे व्यावहारिक प्रश्न उठाउँछ।

अर्को अध्याय ती मानिसतिर जान्छ जसका लागि यो पोसाक लगाइन्छ।
