## [en] The valley above

Dhorpatan is a broad high valley in the Dhaulagiri foothills, ringed by ridges and reached by long walks from the districts around it. For centuries it was a summer pasture rather than a permanent settlement: herders brought animals up when the snow cleared, lived in temporary shelters, made butter and dried cheese, sheared wool and returned before the winter. The valley's grassland, its water and its relative flatness made it one of the most valuable seasonal resources in the region, and rights to use it were held by households and villages that could defend them.

## [ne] माथिको उपत्यका

## [en] The transhumant year

Herding followed a calendar that meshed with farming. In spring, animals were moved up from the villages, sometimes in stages, to graze the newly opened pastures; through the summer they were milked, sheared and guarded against predators; in autumn they came down, and the manure they left on the fields was as important as the milk. Households divided labour: men and boys took the herds, women managed the village farm and processed dairy products, and children learned the routes and the animals' names. The system required agreements about water, grazing and the order of arrival, and those agreements were enforced by village elders rather than by written contracts.

## [ne] आवागमनको वर्ष

गोठालो खेतीसँग मिल्ने पात्रोमा चल्थ्यो। वसन्तमा पशु गाउँबाट माथि, कहिले चरणबद्ध रूपमा, नयाँ खुलेका चरनमा जान्थे; गर्मीभर दुहुन, ऊन काटिन र सिकारीबाट जोगाइन्थे; शरदमा तल झर्थे, र खेतमा छोडेको मल दूधजत्तिकै महत्त्वपूर्ण थियो। घरले श्रम बाँड्थ्यो: पुरुष र केटाले बथान लैजान्थे, महिलाले गाउँको खेत र दुग्ध-प्रशोधन सम्हाल्थे, र बालबालिकाले बाटो र पशुका नाम सिक्थे। व्यवस्थालाई पानी, चरन र आगमन-क्रमका सम्झौता चाहिन्थ्यो, र ती लिखित करारले नभई गाउँका ज्येष्ठले लागू गर्थे।

## [en] Wool, milk and meat

The herd produced more than food. Wool was woven into blankets, pakhi and rope, and sold or bartered for grain; milk became butter, ghee and dried cheese, some of which travelled to markets far below; animals were sold for meat and for sacrifice; and hides, horns and manure had their own uses. A household with a large herd was wealthy in a way that land alone could not express, because animals could be moved, sold and divided more easily than fields. The herding economy and the trade economy were therefore linked: a good year on the pasture meant goods to sell at the pass, and a bad year meant debts that had to be met from the farm.

## [en] The reserve

In 1987 the valley and its surroundings were declared the Dhorpatan Hunting Reserve, the only hunting reserve in Nepal, managed for trophy hunting of blue sheep and other species as well as for conservation. The reserve changed the rules: grazing, cutting and hunting became regulated, permits and fees became part of the local economy, and herders had to negotiate with a new authority. Some households gained work as guides, porters and camp staff; others found their access to pasture restricted. Conservation and herding are not automatically enemies — well-managed grazing can maintain open grassland — but the interests are not identical, and the negotiations are ongoing.

## [en] Herding after the roads

Roads, schools and migration have scattered the arrangement. Young people leave for study and work, and few are willing to spend a summer alone with a herd. Synthetic rope and machine-made blankets have reduced demand for wool, and cheap imported cheese competes with the local product. Some families keep a few animals for milk and ritual use and no longer move them; others have sold the herd altogether. What survives is the knowledge — of routes, weather, plants, animal medicine and the social rules of the pasture — and that knowledge is now held mainly by older people, which is why projects that record it are urgent rather than decorative.
