## [en] Two names, many experiences

Judith Pettigrew, who spent years in Gurung villages recording ritual language, settles the two names into a single phrase on the first page of her work: Tamu (Gurung). The brackets are not a footnote. They are the argument, compressed.

Anyone who has filled in a Nepali form and then stood in a ritual has lived the reason they exist. At the ward office the clerk wants a caste name for the computer, and the name on the citizenship certificate is Gurung. In the courtyard, at a death or a Lhosar, the name spoken is Tamu, and it is spoken by people who would find "Gurung" slightly foreign in that setting — the word a schoolteacher uses, not the word an ancestor answers to.

Neither name is a mistake, and neither is a translation of the other. Gurung belongs to Nepali, and through Nepali to English: it is the name the state writes down, the name in the census tables, the name on the regimental roll. Tamu belongs to the community's own speech, to its organisations, and increasingly to its public voice. The two have coexisted for so long that treating them as a succession — as though one replaced the other on a date that could be looked up — loses the thing that matters. People describe themselves according to where they are, who is asking and what the paper in front of them requires. The same person can be Gurung at the bank and Tamu at a Lhosar committee meeting without feeling any contradiction at all.

That double life is the first thing this book has to explain, because everything that follows — clans, origins, rituals, migration — is described by people who move between the two names daily. And the double life is not only a Nepali story. It travels.

The convention of writing one name and bracketing the other is not peculiar to this community; anthropologists use it whenever a people's own name differs from the one recorded by the state. What makes the Gurung case distinctive is how long both names have been in simultaneous use, and how little either has been able to displace the other. A name that only existed on forms would have faded; a name that only existed in ritual would never have reached the census. Both reached both places, which is why the brackets are still needed.

## [ne] दुई नाम, धेरै अनुभव

तामु कर्मकाण्डी जीवनबारे वर्षौँ गाउँमा बसेर लेख्ने Judith Pettigrew ले आफ्नो कामको पहिलो पृष्ठमै दुई नामलाई एउटै वाक्यांशमा राख्छिन्: तामु (गुरुङ)। कोष्ठकभित्रको कुरा नै सारा तर्क हो, र त्यो कोष्ठकले दुई नामबीचको दूरी र त्यो दूरीभित्र बाँचेको जीवन दुवै देखाउँछ।

जसले नेपाली फारम भरेर पछि कुनै कर्ममा उभिएको छ, उसले ती दुई नाम किन छन् भन्ने आफैँ बाँचेको हुन्छ। वडा कार्यालयमा कर्मचारीलाई कम्प्युटरका लागि जातको नाम चाहिन्छ, र नागरिकतामा लेखिएको नाम गुरुङ हुन्छ। आँगनमा, मृत्यु वा ल्होसारमा, बोलिने नाम तामु हुन्छ, र त्यो नाम त्यहाँ "गुरुङ" लाई अलि परको शब्द ठान्ने मानिसले बोल्छ — विद्यालयका शिक्षकले प्रयोग गर्ने शब्द, पुर्खाले जवाफ नदिने शब्द।

दुवै नाम गल्ती होइनन्, र कुनै पनि अर्काको अनुवाद होइन। गुरुङ नेपालीको हो, र नेपाली हुँदै अङ्ग्रेजीको: राज्यले लेख्ने नाम, जनगणनाको तालिकाको नाम, रेजिमेन्टल सूचीको नाम। तामु समुदायको आफ्नै बोलीको, संस्थाको, र बढ्दो रूपमा सार्वजनिक आवाजको नाम हो।

दुई नाम यति लामो समय सँगै छन् कि तिनलाई एउटाले अर्कोलाई कुनै मितिमा प्रतिस्थापन गरेको क्रम मान्दा महत्त्वपूर्ण कुरा हराउँछ। मानिस आफूलाई कहाँ छन्, कसले सोधिरहेको छ र अगाडि भएको कागजले के माग्छ भन्नेअनुसार वर्णन गर्छ। एउटै मानिस बैंकमा गुरुङ र ल्होसार समितिको बैठकमा तामु हुन सक्छ, र कुनै विरोधाभास महसुस गर्दैन।

यो दोहोरो जीवन नै यो पुस्तकले पहिले बुझाउनुपर्ने कुरा हो, किनकि त्यसपछिका सबै कुरा — क्ले, उत्पत्ति, कर्म, बसाइँसराइ — दुई नामबीच दैनिक सर्ने मानिसले वर्णन गरेका हुन्। र यो दोहोरो जीवन केवल नेपाली कथा पनि होइन; यो यात्रा गर्छ।

एउटा नाम लेखेर अर्को कोष्ठकमा राख्ने चलन यो समुदायको मात्र होइन; मानवशास्त्रीहरूले यो तब प्रयोग गर्छन् जब जनताको आफ्नै नाम राज्यले लेखेको नामभन्दा फरक हुन्छ। गुरुङको हकमा विशेष कुरा यो हो: दुवै नाम कति लामो समय सँगसँगै प्रयोग भए, र एउटाले अर्कोलाई कति कम विस्थापित गर्न सक्यो।

केवल फारममा हुने नाम मेटिँदै जान्थ्यो; केवल कर्ममा हुने नाम कहिल्यै जनगणनासम्म पुग्दैनथ्यो। दुवै दुवै ठाउँमा पुगे, र त्यसैले कोष्ठक अझै चाहिन्छ।

त्यसैले यो अध्याय त्यो खुकुलोपनलाई भत्काउन होइन, बुझ्न लेखिएको हो।

## [en] Belonging in a changing world

Belonging survives distance, but it does not survive unchanged. In Britain, Florence Gurung wrote a doctoral thesis at Oxford on how religious practice and community institutions take part in ethnic belonging among Gurungs, and what she found is far less tidy than a list of customs that must be preserved.

Family connections mattered. So did ancestral places, language and ritual. But they did not matter in the same combination for every household, and they did not matter in the same way for every generation. People who share a surname might keep different festivals, speak different languages at the kitchen table, and disagree — politely, and then less politely — about what a Tamu organisation in Britain is actually for. One family's Lhosar is a temple visit and a meal; another's is a rented hall, a stage and three hundred people.

That looseness is not a failure of identity. It is what identity looks like when it is carried by people rather than stored in a definition. A community that lives in a village can leave the details to the village; a community scattered across Rushmoor, Reading and Kathmandu has to decide, household by household, what to keep and how. Which is why this book says "many" where it could say "the", and why the next section is a warning about reading it.

Her setting matters for how the finding should be read. Rushmoor, the Hampshire district that includes Aldershot, is a garrison town with a Gurkha presence going back decades, and the community she studied lives with British institutions — schools, councils, temples, the army — in a way that a community in Hong Kong or Sikkim does not. The thesis is therefore a careful account of one diaspora situation, not a template for all of them, and it is used here for what it shows about the mechanics of belonging rather than for numbers.

## [ne] बदलिँदो संसारमा अपनत्व

बेलायतमा Florence Gurung ले अक्सफोर्डमा गुरुङबीच धार्मिक अभ्यास र सामुदायिक संस्थाले जातीय अपनत्वमा कसरी भाग लिन्छन् भन्ने अध्ययन गरिन्, र उनले भेटेको कुरा जोगाउनुपर्ने चलनको सूचीभन्दा धेरै कम सफा छ।

पारिवारिक सम्बन्ध महत्त्वपूर्ण थिए। पुर्ख्यौली ठाउँ, भाषा र कर्म पनि। तर ती सबैका लागि एउटै संयोजनमा होइन, र हरेक पुस्ताका लागि एउटै तरिकाले होइन। एउटै थर बाँड्ने मानिसले फरक चाड राख्न सक्छन्, भान्सामा फरक भाषा बोल्न सक्छन्, र बेलायतमा तामु संस्था केका लागि हो भन्नेमा — पहिले भद्र र पछि कम भद्र — असहमत हुन सक्छन्। एउटा परिवारको ल्होसार मन्दिर-भ्रमण र खाना हो; अर्काको भाडाको हल, मञ्च र तीन सय मानिस।

त्यो खुकुलोपन पहिचानको असफलता होइन। पहिचान परिभाषामा भण्डारण नभई मानिसले बोक्दा यस्तै देखिन्छ। गाउँमा बस्ने समुदायले विवरण गाउँलाई छाड्न सक्छ; Rushmoor, Reading र काठमाडौँमा छरिएको समुदायले घर-घरमै के राख्ने र कसरी राख्ने तय गर्नुपर्छ। त्यसैले यो पुस्तकले "त्यो" भन्न सक्ने ठाउँमा "धेरै" भन्छ।

उनको सेटिङले निष्कर्ष कसरी पढ्ने भन्ने पनि तोक्छ। Rushmoor, जसमा Aldershot पर्छ, दशकौँदेखि गोर्खा उपस्थिति भएको छाउनी जिल्ला हो, र उनले अध्ययन गरेको समुदाय बेलायती संस्था — विद्यालय, परिषद्, मन्दिर, सेना — सँग बाँच्छ, जसरी हङकङ वा सिक्किमको समुदाय बाँच्दैन। त्यसैले प्रबन्ध एउटा प्रवास-स्थितिको होसियार वर्णन हो, सबैका लागि ढाँचा होइन।

## [en] A note on interpretation

This introduction does not propose one universal model of Tamu identity, and it would be dishonest to pretend that one exists. Community accounts and historical records are read alongside each other here, with attention to region, generation and the situation in which an ethnic name is used. A family in Lamjung, a family in Rushmoor and a family in Sikkim may share a clan and disagree about almost everything else — about which festivals matter, which language children should learn first, and whether the community's future lies in the hills or in the cities.

Where the sources disagree, the disagreement stays visible rather than being smoothed into a sentence that pleases everyone. That is a deliberate choice, and it costs something: a reader who wants a single clean answer will not always get one. What they will get instead is the state of the evidence, which is more useful, and which is the only honest way to write about a community this large and this spread out.

The first disagreement is the name itself — and it is older than any of the others.

For a reader this has a practical consequence. You will meet hedges in this book — "in some villages", "the sources disagree", "this cannot be dated" — and they are not evasion. They are the places where the evidence genuinely stops, and marking them is what allows the rest of the account to be trusted. A book that smoothed every uncertainty into a confident sentence would read more smoothly and teach less.

## [ne] व्याख्याबारे एउटा टिप्पणी

यो परिचयले तामु पहिचानको एउटै सार्वभौम ढाँचा प्रस्ताव गर्दैन, र त्यस्तो ढाँचा छ भन्नु इमानदारी हुँदैन। यहाँ सामुदायिक विवरण र ऐतिहासिक अभिलेख सँगै पढिन्छन्, क्षेत्र, पुस्ता र जातीय नाम प्रयोग हुने परिवेशमा ध्यान दिँदै। लमजुङको परिवार, Rushmoor को परिवार र सिक्किमको परिवार एउटै क्ले बाँड्न सक्छन् र अरू सबै कुरामा असहमत हुन सक्छन् — कुन चाड महत्त्वपूर्ण, बालबालिकाले पहिले कुन भाषा सिक्ने, र समुदायको भविष्य पहाडमा हो कि सहरमा।

ती स्रोत असहमत हुँदा असहमति सबैलाई खुसी पार्ने वाक्यमा मिलाइँदैन, देखिने गरी राखिन्छ। त्यो जानाजान गरिएको छनोट हो, र त्यसको मूल्य छ: एउटै सफा जवाफ चाहने पाठकले सधैँ नपाउन सक्छ। पाउने कुरा प्रमाणको अवस्था हो, जो बढी उपयोगी छ, र यति ठूलो र यति छरिएको समुदायबारे लेख्ने एउटै इमानदार तरिका हो।

पहिलो असहमति नाम आफैँमा छ, र त्यो अरू सबैभन्दा पुरानो छ।

पाठकका लागि यसको व्यावहारिक परिणाम छ। यो पुस्तकमा तपाईं "केही गाउँमा", "स्रोत असहमत छन्", "यसको मिति तोक्न सकिँदैन" जस्ता शब्द भेट्नुहुनेछ, र ती टार्ने तरिका होइनन्। ती त्यो ठाउँ हुन् जहाँ प्रमाण साँच्चै रोकिन्छ, र तिनलाई चिन्ह लगाउँदा बाँकी वर्णनमा भरोसा गर्न सकिन्छ। हरेक अनिश्चिततालाई आत्मविश्वासी वाक्यमा समथर बनाउने पुस्तक राम्रोसँग पढिन्थ्यो र कम सिकाउँथ्यो।

## [en] Where the name Gurung comes from

The name on a citizenship certificate is Gurung. The name spoken in a ritual is Tamu. Gurung is not the community's own name for itself: it belongs to Nepali and, through Nepali, to English, and its origin is disputed. Some accounts derive it from a place, some from an occupation or a clan label, some from the way neighbouring peoples described the community. Folk etymologies are plentiful, and none is firmly established.

What is clear is the direction of travel. "Gurung" is the name by which the community became known to the Nepali state, to British recruiters and to the wider world; it is the name that appears in nineteenth-century gazetteers, in army records and in the earliest ethnographies. "Tamu" is the name the community uses for itself, and it appears in the ritual vocabulary, in the names of organisations founded in the twentieth century, and now on festival banners.

Tamu has a different kind of life from Gurung. It carries the sense of a people speaking to itself, and it is the word that makes a sentence like "we are Tamu" feel like a statement of fact rather than a label on a form. Two names have coexisted for a long time, which is exactly what happens when a people deals with states, armies and neighbours in one language while speaking to itself in another. The double life is not a recent invention, and it is not a fashion among activists. It is the oldest thing about the names.

The English spelling has its own small history. "Gurung" is a transliteration that became fixed through colonial gazetteers, army rolls and missionary printing, and it has stayed fixed long enough to be treated as the English name of the community. It also became a surname: today a Gurung in Britain or America is likely to be recorded as "Gurung" in the surname field, whatever clan the family actually belongs to. The clan name, which carries the real information, is often the one that gets dropped.

## [ne] गुरुङ नाम कहाँबाट आयो

नागरिकतामा लेखिएको नाम गुरुङ हो। कर्ममा बोलिने नाम तामु हो। गुरुङ समुदायको आफ्नै नाम होइन: यो नेपालीको हो र नेपाली हुँदै अङ्ग्रेजीको, र यसको उत्पत्ति विवादित छ। कसैले ठाउँको नामबाट, कसैले पेसा वा क्लेको लेबलबाट, कसैले छिमेकी जनताले समुदायलाई वर्णन गरेको तरिकाबाट व्युत्पन्न भएको भन्छन्। लोक-व्युत्पत्ति धेरै छन् र कुनै पनि पक्का छैन।

स्पष्ट कुरा यात्राको दिशा हो। "गुरुङ" त्यो नाम हो जसबाट समुदाय नेपाली राज्य, बेलायती भर्तीकर्ता र फराकिलो संसारसामु चिनियो; त्यो नाम उन्नाइसौँ शताब्दीका गजेटियर, सैनिक अभिलेख र सुरुका नृवंश-विवरणमा छ। "तामु" समुदायले आफ्ना लागि प्रयोग गर्ने नाम हो, र त्यो कर्मकाण्डी शब्दावलीमा, बीसौँ शताब्दीमा स्थापित संस्थाका नाममा, र अहिले चाडका ब्यानरमा देखिन्छ।

तामुको जीवन गुरुङभन्दा फरक किसिमको छ। यसले आफैँसँग बोल्ने जनताको भाव बोक्छ, र यसैले "हामी तामु हौँ" भन्ने वाक्य फारमको लेबल होइन, तथ्यको भनाइजस्तो सुनिन्छ। दुई नाम लामो समय सँगै छन्, र राज्य, सेना र छिमेकीसँग एउटा भाषामा व्यवहार गर्दै आफूसँग अर्को भाषामा बोल्ने जनताको हकमा ठ्याक्कै त्यही हुन्छ। यो दोहोरो जीवन नयाँ आविष्कार होइन, न कार्यकर्ताबीचको फेसन। यो नामबारेको सबैभन्दा पुरानो कुरा हो।

अङ्ग्रेजी हिज्जेको आफ्नै सानो इतिहास छ। "Gurung" त्यो ट्रान्सलिटरेसन हो जो औपनिवेशिक गजेटियर, सैनिक सूची र मिसनरी छपाइबाट स्थिर भयो, र यति लामो समय स्थिर रह्यो कि यसलाई समुदायको अङ्ग्रेजी नाम मानिन्छ। यो थर पनि बन्यो: आज बेलायत वा अमेरिकाको गुरुङको थर-फाँटमा "Gurung" नै लेखिन्छ, परिवार कुन क्लेको भए पनि। वास्तविक सूचना बोक्ने क्लेको नाम प्रायः छुट्ने नाम हो।

## [en] Tamu in the census and in the movement

Official Nepal uses Gurung as the census category. The 2021 census form recorded Gurung identity and the Gurung language under that name, and every development programme, scholarship quota and official list works from that same word.

Since the 1990s, however, many organisations have preferred Tamu. The shift came with the wider indigenous-rights movement in Nepal, in which communities began to argue that the state's names for them were either external labels or misspellings, and that self-designation should be recognised. Some activists have campaigned for the official name to change, or for Tamu to be recognised alongside Gurung. The argument is partly about self-definition and partly about the state's power to name: who decides what a community is called in the records that allocate rights?

That question is not academic. A name in a census table becomes a budget line, a quota, a language in a school curriculum. To be counted under one name and to call yourself another is to be, in a small but real way, governed by someone else's vocabulary. And yet the campaign has not produced a simple replacement. In practice both terms are used. A government office will say Gurung, a community council may say Tamu, and the same person may use either depending on the form they are filling in. Ethnic labels are negotiated rather than fixed — and the negotiation does not stop at the border.

The shift towards Tamu belongs to a wider movement. After the restoration of democracy in 1990, Nepal's indigenous nationalities — janajati — organised in a way that had not been possible before, and a generation of activists began to argue that the state's ethnic categories were both incomplete and badly spelled. For Gurungs this produced organisations, festivals, publications and a vocabulary of self-description that reached villages and diaspora simultaneously. The census category did not change, but what people called themselves in public did.

## [ne] जनगणना र आन्दोलनमा तामु

आधिकारिक नेपालले जनगणनाको कोटिका रूपमा गुरुङ प्रयोग गर्छ। सन् २०२१ को जनगणना-फारमले त्यही नाममुनि गुरुङ पहिचान तथा गुरुङ भाषा रेकर्ड गर्‍यो, र हरेक विकास-कार्यक्रम, छात्रवृत्ति-कोटा र आधिकारिक सूची त्यही शब्दबाट चल्छ।

तर १९९० को दशकदेखि धेरै संस्थाले तामु रुचाएका छन्। परिवर्तन नेपालको फराकिलो आदिवासी-अधिकार आन्दोलनसँग आयो, जसमा समुदायले राज्यले दिएका नाम बाहिरी लेबल वा गलत हिज्जे भएको र आत्म-नामकरण मान्य हुनुपर्ने तर्क गर्न थाले। केही कार्यकर्ताले आधिकारिक नाम बदल्न वा गुरुङसँगै तामु मान्यता दिन अभियान चलाए। बहस आत्म-परिभाषा र राज्यको नामकरण गर्ने शक्तिबारे दुवै हो: अधिकार बाँड्ने अभिलेखमा समुदायलाई के भनिने, त्यो कसले तय गर्ने?

त्यो प्रश्न शैक्षिक होइन। जनगणनाको तालिकाको नाम बजेट-लाइन, कोटा र विद्यालयको पाठ्यक्रमको भाषा बन्छ। एउटा नाममुनि गनिनु र आफूलाई अर्को नाम भन्नु भनेको सानो तर साँचो रूपमा अरू कसैको शब्दावलीले शासित हुनु हो। तैपनि अभियानले सरल प्रतिस्थापन निकालेन। व्यवहारमा दुवै शब्द प्रयोग हुन्छन्: सरकारी कार्यालयले गुरुङ भन्छ, सामुदायिक परिषद्ले तामु भन्न सक्छ, र एउटै व्यक्तिले भर्ने फारमअनुसार दुवै प्रयोग गर्छ। जातीय लेबल स्थिर होइन, मोलमोलाइ हुन्छ — र त्यो मोलमोलाइ सीमापारि पनि जारी रहन्छ।

तामुतिरको झुकाव फराकिलो आन्दोलनको भाग हो। सन् १९९० मा लोकतन्त्र पुनर्स्थापित भएपछि नेपालका आदिवासी जनजाति यसअघि नभएको तरिकाले संगठित भए, र कार्यकर्ताको एउटा पुस्ताले राज्यका जातीय कोटि अधुरा र गलत हिज्जेका छन् भन्न थाले। गुरुङका लागि यसले संस्था, चाड, प्रकाशन र आत्म-वर्णनको शब्दावली निकाल्यो, जो गाउँ र प्रवासमा एकैचोटि पुग्यो। जनगणनाको कोटि बदलिएन, तर मानिसले सार्वजनिक रूपमा आफूलाई के भन्ने भन्ने बदलियो।

## [en] Names in a diaspora

A family that arrives in Britain or the United States must fit its names into passport systems, school registers and payroll databases, which often have no field for a clan and no patience for spelling variation.

Gurung becomes a surname even when it was a category. Clan names move to middle names or disappear entirely, because a form asks for a first name and a last name and nothing else. Children are given names that work in English and Nepali at once — a name that a teacher can pronounce and a grandmother can recognise — and parents negotiate over which language the name should come from.

Some go the other way and choose Tamu names deliberately, sometimes reviving an older name from the family's village. Community organisations press for the correct spelling of clan names in directories, on memorials and in the programmes of Lhosar events, because a misspelled clan name is not a typo to the family that carries it. Naming is one of the quiet places where a diaspora both loses and asserts its heritage: every school register is a small act of translation, and every correctly spelled clan name on a memorial is a small act of recovery.

What those surnames carry — the clans themselves, their rules and their quarrels — is the subject of the next chapter.

By the third generation the arithmetic has changed again. A child born in Britain may hold a British birth certificate, an English first name, a Nepali middle name and a surname that was originally a category. Parents choose which parts to keep and which to translate, and they do it knowing that a name is the one piece of heritage a school register will carry every day. Some families keep a Tamu name for home and an English one for school; others insist on one name everywhere and accept the mispronunciation as a small tax on belonging.

## [ne] प्रवासमा नामहरू

बेलायत वा अमेरिका पुगेको परिवारले आफ्ना नाम राहदानी प्रणाली, विद्यालय दर्ता र तलब-डेटाबेसमा मिलाउनुपर्छ, जहाँ प्रायः क्लेका लागि फाँट हुँदैन र हिज्जेको भिन्नताका लागि धैर्य हुँदैन।

गुरुङ कोटि हुँदाहुँदै उपनाम बन्छ। क्लेको नाम बीचको नाममा सर्छ वा पूरै हराउँछ, किनकि फारमले पहिलो र अन्तिम नाम मात्र सोध्छ। बालबालिकालाई अङ्ग्रेजी र नेपाली दुवैमा चल्ने नाम दिइन्छ — शिक्षकले उच्चारण गर्न सक्ने र हजुरआमाले चिन्न सक्ने — र आमाबुबा नाम कुन भाषाबाट आउने भन्नेमा मोलमोलाइ गर्छन्।

केही उल्टो बाटो लिन्छन् र जानाजान तामु नाम छान्छन्, कहिलेकाहीँ परिवारको गाउँको पुरानो नाम फर्काएर। सामुदायिक संस्थाले निर्देशिका, स्मारक र ल्होसार कार्यक्रममा क्लेको नामको शुद्ध हिज्जेका लागि दबाब दिन्छन्, किनकि गलत हिज्जे भएको क्लेको नाम त्यो बोक्ने परिवारका लागि टाइपो होइन। नामकरण प्रवासले सम्पदा हराउने र दाबी गर्ने शान्त तरिकामध्ये एक हो: हरेक विद्यालय-दर्ता अनुवादको सानो कर्म हो, र स्मारकमा शुद्ध लेखिएको हरेक क्लेको नाम पुनःप्राप्तिको सानो कर्म।

ती उपनामले बोकेको कुरा — क्लेहरू, तिनका नियम र झगडा — अर्को अध्यायको विषय हो।

तेस्रो पुस्तासम्म हिसाब फेरि बदलिन्छ। बेलायतमा जन्मेको बालबालिकासँग बेलायती जन्म-प्रमाणपत्र, अङ्ग्रेजी पहिलो नाम, नेपाली बीचको नाम र मूलतः कोटि रहेको थर हुन सक्छ। आमाबुबाले कुन भाग राख्ने र कुन अनुवाद गर्ने छान्छन्, र छान्दा थाहा पाउँछन् कि नाम नै त्यो सम्पदा हो जो विद्यालय-दर्ताले दैनिक बोक्छ। केही परिवारले घरमा तामु नाम र विद्यालयमा अङ्ग्रेजी नाम राख्छन्; अरूले सबै ठाउँमा एउटै नाम लिन्छन् र गलत उच्चारणलाई अपनत्वको सानो कर मान्छन्।
