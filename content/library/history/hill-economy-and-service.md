## [en] The economy that came first

The history of Gurung military service is usually told as a story about regiments: recruitment, campaigns, medals, pensions. That story is real, but it begins in a household economy that had nothing to do with British India and everything to do with altitude, land and obligation. Before a regiment existed, Gurung families were already balancing fields, animals, forests, trade and the demands of a state that taxed them. Understanding that balance is necessary for understanding why service became attractive, what it cost, and how it changed the hills.

The homeland is a landscape of sharp differences. In the valleys there is irrigated land, the khet, where rice can be grown where water allows. On the slopes there are dry terraces, the bari, producing maize, millet, buckwheat, barley, pulses and vegetables. Above the fields are forests for fuel, fodder and grazing, and above them the high pastures that open in summer. A household that relied on one zone alone would be vulnerable to a bad year; a household that could move between zones had a measure of security. The economy of the Gurung hills was therefore vertical before it was commercial.

Mahesh Chandra Regmi's work on Nepali economic history shows how the state fitted into this landscape. His study of the period from 1768 to 1846 uses land and revenue records to reconstruct a system in which households were assessed for tax, land was granted to officials and soldiers in return for service, and local intermediaries mediated between the state and the village. For a Gurung family, the state was not an abstract authority. It was a demand for grain, labour or money, delivered through people the family knew.

## [en] Terraces, crops and the discipline of the slope

Terraced farming is often described as if it were a static inheritance from the distant past. In fact it is a continuous work of construction and repair. A terrace is a wall, a level surface and a channel; it has to be maintained after every monsoon, and a single breach can carry away a season's soil. Families invested labour in terraces because land was the only reliable form of wealth, and because land was also the basis of status and of obligations to the state and to kin.

The crop calendar shaped everything else. Rice in the irrigated fields, maize and millet on the dry slopes, buckwheat and barley at higher elevations, pulses and vegetables in the gaps: each crop had its own season, its own labour demands and its own risk. Harvests were staggered so that labour could be shared, and the whole village moved through the year in a sequence that was also a ritual calendar. The festivals that outsiders described as custom were, among other things, markers of agricultural time.

Animal manure, forest litter and the grazing of stubble kept the fields productive, which is why the forest and the pasture were not "wilderness" in the modern sense but part of the farm. A family's rights to graze, to collect fodder and to take fuel were as important as its title to a field, and disputes over those rights were among the most common conflicts in village life. Messerschmidt's study of a Gurung village documents how such conflicts were handled, and how they changed as the state and the market reached further into the hills.

## [en] Transhumance and the high pastures

Herding was not a separate occupation from farming; it was the upper storey of the same household economy. Families kept cattle and buffalo for milk, manure and ploughing, goats and sheep for meat, wool and sale, and in some areas larger herds that were moved seasonally to high pastures. The pattern is known as transhumance: animals and the people who tended them moved up in summer and down in winter, following grass and avoiding the worst of the cold.

The high pastures were used by particular families and villages under arrangements that were partly customary and partly negotiated. A herder needed access to water, shelter and salt, and the routes between the low village and the high pasture crossed land used by others. Transhumance therefore required agreement, and the agreements were part of the social fabric: who could use which pasture, for how long, with how many animals, and what was owed to the owner of the land. These arrangements were rarely written down, and they are one of the parts of the old economy that is hardest to reconstruct.

Herding also connected the hills to wider markets. Wool, butter, cheese, hides and live animals moved down to the bazaars and up to the trade routes, and the proceeds helped a household pay taxes, buy salt, cloth and metal goods, and meet the costs of marriage and ritual. A family with animals had a cushion against a bad harvest; a family without them was more exposed. The difference between the two was one of the main lines of inequality within a Gurung village.

## [en] Salt, wool and the trade routes

The northern trade was the commercial layer of this economy. For centuries, salt from Tibet, wool and butter from the plateau, grain and chillies from the middle hills, and manufactured goods from the Indian plains moved along routes that crossed the Gurung homeland. The Kyirong corridor was one of the most important, and the villages along it were not merely passing places: they provided porters, pack animals, food, shelter and credit, and their households were shaped by the traffic.

Trade was seasonal and risky. Caravans moved when passes were open, prices varied with political conditions, and a war or a border closure could cut a route for years. The Nepal–Tibet trade treaties and the regulations that governed them are part of the documentary history of the region, and they show how much the hill economy depended on connections that reached far beyond the village. A household that could combine farming, herding and a share in trade was more secure than one that depended on a single source.

The trade also carried ideas and people. Monasteries, pilgrims, traders, soldiers and officials moved along the same routes, and the northern villages of the homeland became a zone of contact between Tibetan and Nepali worlds. That contact is visible in language, ritual and material culture, and it is one reason the Gurung past cannot be understood as an isolated hill story. The economy was connected long before the regiments arrived.

## [en] Obligations to the state

The state's demands took several forms. Land revenue was assessed on households and collected in grain, cash or both. Labour could be required for public works, for the transport of goods and officials, and for the provisioning of troops. Certain lands were granted to officials or soldiers in return for service, and the people who farmed them owed their obligations to the grant holder rather than directly to the state. Regmi's documents show a system that was complex, locally variable and often harsh, and they show households negotiating, evading and resisting its demands.

For Gurung history, the important point is that military service did not arrive from nowhere in the nineteenth century. The Gorkha state raised soldiers from the hill communities it conquered and incorporated, and service was tied to land, status and obligation. The library's account of Gorkha service notes that documentary research suggests the early Gorkha state recruited mainly Magars and Khasas, and that Gurungs from Lamjung entered the army more gradually as the kingdom absorbed the territories in which they lived. Service was one form of the relationship between a household and a state, alongside tax and labour.

That relationship explains why the British recruiting system found ready ground. A family already used to providing men for a ruler, in exchange for land or status, could understand a regiment's terms. What changed was the scale, the regularity and the money. The Company and later the Crown offered cash wages and pensions, and those were something the hill states had rarely provided in the same form.

## [en] The Company's terms and the regimental economy

British recruitment of Gurkhas began after the Anglo-Nepal war of 1814–16, when the East India Company, impressed by the hill soldiers it had fought, began to enlist them. The early terms were not generous by modern standards, but they were regular: pay, food, clothing, medical care in some form, and the promise of a pension after long service. For a hill household, a regular cash income was a new kind of resource, and it changed what a family could do with its land and labour.

Recruiting was organised through depots and officers who returned to the same districts year after year. Vansittart's handbook describes the machinery: which areas were considered good recruiting ground, how recruits were attested, what customs officers should expect. Northey and Morris, writing later, describe the regimental world that had grown up around those arrangements, including the expectations placed on soldiers' families. The regiment was not only a military unit; it was an institution with its own rituals, hierarchies and networks, and those networks reached into the hills.

The economics were double-edged. A serving soldier's pay arrived in cash, which could be used to buy land, pay a debt, fund a marriage or educate a child. A pension provided security in old age, which few hill families had. But service removed a man from the household for years, and the household had to cover his share of the agricultural work. Women often ran the farm, managed the animals and dealt with officials while their husbands were away, and the household's success depended on their labour as much as on the soldier's wage.

## [en] The household as the unit of strategy

The most important insight of the economic history is that the decision to enlist was usually a household decision, not an individual one. A family with several sons might send one to the regiment and keep the others on the land, using the wage to buy more land or to pay for a brother's education. A family with debts might send a son to clear them. A family with no sons might arrange for a nephew to go. The regiment offered a route to cash and status, and the household calculated how to use it.

This calculation shaped the pattern of recruitment. Districts with good land and strong households could afford to send men and to benefit from their wages; districts with less land were more dependent on service. Over time, service became part of the local culture of certain areas, and a family's reputation could be tied to its record of military service. That culture was not invented by the British; it grew out of the older relationship between hill households and states, and it was reinforced by the pensions and privileges that service brought.

The costs were also distributed through the household. A man who died or was disabled left a family dependent on a pension that might be difficult to claim. A long absence could strain a marriage and change the balance of authority within the household. And the money that came home was not always spent on the farm: it could be invested in trade, in a house, in a son's education or in the ritual obligations that maintained the family's standing. The regimental economy was woven into the village economy, not separate from it.

## [en] Change in the twentieth century

The twentieth century brought the two world wars, which drew Gurung soldiers into conflicts far from the hills and brought new experiences, new skills and new losses. It brought the end of Rana rule in 1951, the opening of Nepal to development, and the spread of schools, roads and government offices. It brought tourism to villages such as Ghandruk, and later migration to Kathmandu, Pokhara, India, Britain, Hong Kong and the United States.

Each of these changes altered the household economy. Schools created a new kind of investment and a new route out of farming. Roads reduced the cost of transport and weakened the old trade routes. Tourism created a market for lodges, guides and porters, and it changed the value of houses and land in the villages that visitors reached. Migration produced remittances that replaced or supplemented military pay. The old balance between fields, herds, trade and service was gradually replaced by a more diverse and more cash-dependent economy.

The long-term record assembled by Harrison and Macfarlane shows how uneven this change was. Some villages acquired roads and schools decades before others. Some families moved into tourism or the professions while others remained dependent on farming and remittances. The pattern of inequality that had existed in the old economy — between families with animals and land and those without — did not disappear; it was reorganised around new resources. The history of the Gurung economy is therefore not a story of transition from tradition to modernity, but of repeated reorganisations of the same household problem: how to secure a living from an uncertain landscape.

## [en] What the economy explains about Gurung history

The economic history explains several things that a purely military or cultural account cannot. It explains why military service was attractive without being irrational: a regular wage and a pension were genuinely rare goods in the hill economy. It explains why service was a household strategy rather than an individual adventure, and why women's labour was central to it. It explains why the regiments became one of the most important institutions in the Gurung hills long before anyone wrote a history of the Gurungs.

It also explains why the older economic order was vulnerable. A household that depended on seasonal pastures, customary rights and trade routes could be disrupted by a road, a border closure, a change in land law or a new market. The twentieth century brought all of these, and the result was not the disappearance of the old economy but its gradual subordination to a cash economy centred on the state, the army, tourism and remittances.

For the archive, the lesson is that Gurung history cannot be written as a sequence of kings and campaigns. The deep past is a history of households working a vertical landscape, negotiating with states, moving animals and goods, and adapting when the terms changed. The regiments are part of that history, not a separate chapter bolted onto it. And the household — with its fields, its animals, its obligations and its women's work — is the unit that makes the whole story intelligible.

## [ne] खेत, बाली र भिरालोको अनुशासन

खेतको कामलाई प्रायः टाढाको अतीतबाट आएको स्थिर सम्पत्ति जस्तो वर्णन गरिन्छ। वास्तवमा यो निरन्तर निर्माण र मर्मतको काम हो। खेत एउटा पर्खाल, समतल सतह र पानीको धार हो; हरेक वर्षापछि यसको मर्मत गर्नुपर्छ, र एउटा चिराले वर्षभरिको माटो बगाउन सक्छ। परिवारले खेतमा श्रम लगाए किनकि जग्गा नै भरपर्दो सम्पत्ति थियो, र जग्गा हैसियत तथा राज्य र नातेदारप्रतिको दायित्वको आधार पनि थियो।

बालीको पात्रोले अरू सबै आकार दियो। पानी पुगेको खेतमा धान, सुख्खा भिरालोमा मकै र कोदो, उच्च ठाउँमा फापर र जौ, बीचको ठाउँमा दाल र तरकारी: हरेक बालीको आफ्नै ऋतु, श्रम-माग र जोखिम थियो। फसल फरक समयमा पाक्थ्यो ताकि श्रम बाँड्न सकियोस्, र पूरै गाउँ वर्षभरि यस्तो क्रममा चल्थ्यो जो कर्मकाण्डी पात्रो पनि थियो। बाहिरीले चलन भनेर वर्णन गरेका चाड, अरू कुरासँगै, कृषि-समयका चिन्ह थिए।

पशुको मल, वनको सडेको पात र खेतमा छाडिएको डाँठले जमिन उर्वर राख्थ्यो, त्यसैले वन र चरन आधुनिक अर्थमा "जङ्गल" होइन, खेतीकै भाग थिए। परिवारको चरन, घाँस र इन्धनको अधिकार जग्गाको स्वामित्वजत्तिकै महत्त्वपूर्ण थियो, र ती अधिकारका विवाद गाउँ-जीवनका सबैभन्दा सामान्य झगडामध्ये थिए। मेसर्समिटको गुरुङ गाउँ अध्ययनले त्यस्ता विवाद कसरी सुल्झाइन्थे, र राज्य तथा बजार पहाडभित्र पस्दै जाँदा ती कसरी बदलिए, अभिलेख गर्छ।

रेग्मीको कामले राज्य यो भूदृश्यमा कसरी बस्यो देखाउँछ: घरपरिवारमाथि कर, सेवाको बदलामा अधिकारी र सिपाहीलाई जग्गा, र राज्य तथा गाउँबीच मध्यस्थता गर्ने स्थानीय बिचौलिया। त्यसैले जग्गा केवल उत्पादनको साधन थिएन, राज्यसँगको सम्बन्धको मुद्रा पनि थियो।

## [ne] पशुचरन र उच्च चरन

उच्च चरन खास परिवार र गाउँले आंशिक चलन र आंशिक मोलमोलाइका आधारमा प्रयोग गर्थे। गोठालालाई पानी, ओत र नुन चाहिन्थ्यो, र तल्लो गाउँबाट उच्च चरनसम्मको बाटो अरूको जग्गा भएर जान्थ्यो। त्यसैले पशुचरनलाई सहमति चाहिन्थ्यो, र ती सहमति सामाजिक तन्त्रको भाग थिए: कसले कुन चरन, कति समय, कति पशुसहित प्रयोग गर्ने, र जग्गाधनीलाई के दिने। यी व्यवस्था विरलै लेखिएका थिए, र पुरानो अर्थतन्त्रको पुनर्निर्माण गर्न सबैभन्दा कठिन भागमध्ये एक हुन्।

पशुपालनले पहाडलाई फराकिलो बजारसँग पनि जोड्यो। ऊन, घिउ, चिज, छाला र जिउँदा पशु बजारतिर र व्यापार-बाटोतिर जान्थे, र त्यसबाट आएको आयले घरपरिवारलाई कर तिर्न, नुन, कपडा र धातुका सामान किन्न, र विवाह तथा कर्मकाण्डको खर्च धान्न मद्दत गर्थ्यो। पशु भएको परिवारसँग नराम्रो फसलविरुद्ध सुरक्षा थियो; नभएको परिवार बढी जोखिममा पर्थ्यो। यही भिन्नता गुरुङ गाउँभित्रको असमानताका मुख्य रेखामध्ये थियो।

पशुपालन खेतीभन्दा अलग पेसा थिएन; त्यो उही घरेलु अर्थतन्त्रको माथिल्लो तल्ला थियो। परिवारले दूध, मल र हलोका लागि गाई र भैँसी, मासु, ऊन र बिक्रीका लागि बाख्रा र भेडा, र कतै ठूला बथान राख्थे जो ऋतुअनुसार उच्च चरनमा सारिन्थे। यो ढाँचालाई पशुचरन भनिन्छ: पशु र तिनलाई चराउने मानिस गर्मीमा माथि, जाडोमा तल सर्छन्, घाँस पछ्याउँदै र कठोर जाडोबाट बच्दै।

## [ne] नुन, ऊन र व्यापार-बाटो

उत्तरी व्यापार यो अर्थतन्त्रको व्यापारिक तह थियो। शताब्दीयौँ तिब्बतको नुन, पठारको ऊन र घिउ, मध्य पहाडको अन्न र खुर्सानी, र भारतको मैदानका उत्पादन गुरुङ मातृभूमि भएर जाने बाटोमा ओसारिए। क्यिरोङ कोरिडोर सबैभन्दा महत्त्वपूर्णमध्ये एक थियो, र त्यस किनारका गाउँ केवल बाटोका ठाउँ थिएनन्: तिनले भरिया, पशु, खाना, ओत र ऋण दिन्थे, र तिनका घरपरिवार आवागमनले आकार पाएका थिए।

व्यापार ऋतुअनुसार र जोखिमपूर्ण थियो। काफिला भञ्ज्याङ खुला हुँदा हिँड्थे, मूल्य राजनीतिक अवस्थाअनुसार बदलिन्थ्यो, र युद्ध वा सीमा बन्दले बाटो वर्षौँ काट्न सक्थ्यो। नेपाल–तिब्बत व्यापार-सन्धि र तिनलाई नियमित गर्ने कानुन क्षेत्रको दस्तावेजी इतिहासको भाग हुन्, र तिनले पहाडी अर्थतन्त्र गाउँभन्दा धेरै टाढासम्म जोडिएकोमा कति निर्भर थियो देखाउँछन्। खेती, पशुपालन र व्यापारमा हिस्सा जोड्न सक्ने परिवार एउटै स्रोतमा भर पर्नेभन्दा सुरक्षित थियो। व्यापारले विचार, गुम्बा, तीर्थयात्री र मानिस पनि बोक्यो।

व्यापारले विचार र मानिस पनि बोक्यो। गुम्बा, तीर्थयात्री, व्यापारी, सिपाही र अधिकारी उही बाटोमा हिँडे, र मातृभूमिका उत्तरी गाउँ तिब्बती र नेपाली संसारबीचको सम्पर्क-क्षेत्र बने। त्यो सम्पर्क भाषा, कर्मकाण्ड र भौतिक संस्कृतिमा देखिन्छ, र गुरुङ अतीतलाई अलग पहाडी कथा मान्न नहुने एउटा कारण पनि यही हो। रेजिमेन्ट आउनुभन्दा धेरै अघि नै अर्थतन्त्र जोडिएको थियो।

पशुपालनले पहाडलाई फराकिलो बजारसँग जोड्यो। ऊन, घिउ, चिज, छाला र जिउँदा पशु बजारतिर र व्यापार-बाटोतिर जान्थे, र त्यसबाट आएको आयले कर तिर्न, नुन, कपडा र धातुका सामान किन्न, र विवाह तथा कर्मकाण्डको खर्च धान्न मद्दत गर्थ्यो। पशु भएको परिवारसँग नराम्रो फसलविरुद्ध सुरक्षा थियो; नभएको बढी जोखिममा पर्थ्यो — यही भिन्नता गाउँभित्रको असमानताको मुख्य रेखा थियो।

## [ne] राज्यप्रतिको दायित्व

राज्यका माग धेरै रूपमा आउँथे। जग्गाको राजस्व घरपरिवारमाथि लाग्थ्यो र अन्न, नगद वा दुवैमा उठाइन्थ्यो। सार्वजनिक काम, सामान र अधिकारीको ढुवानी, र सैनिकको खानपानका लागि श्रम माग गर्न सकिन्थ्यो। केही जग्गा अधिकारी वा सिपाहीलाई सेवाको बदलामा दिइन्थ्यो, र ती जोतेर खाने मानिसले राज्यलाई सिधै होइन, जग्गा पाउनेलाई दायित्व तिर्थे। रेग्मीका कागजातले जटिल, स्थानीय रूपमा फरक र प्रायः कठोर व्यवस्था देखाउँछन्, र घरपरिवारले ती मागसँग मोलमोलाइ, छल र प्रतिरोध गरेको पनि देखाउँछन्।

गुरुङ इतिहासका लागि महत्त्वपूर्ण कुरा यो हो: सैन्य सेवा उन्नाइसौँ शताब्दीमा अचानक आएको होइन। गोर्खा राज्यले आफूले जितेर आफूमा मिलाएका पहाडी समुदायबाट सिपाही उठाउँथ्यो, र सेवा जग्गा, हैसियत र दायित्वसँग बाँधिएको थियो। गोर्खा सेवाबारे यस पुस्तकालयको लेखले भन्छ, कागजातीय अनुसन्धानअनुसार प्रारम्भिक गोर्खा राज्यले मुख्यतः मगर र खस भर्ना गर्थ्यो, र लमजुङका गुरुङ राज्यले तिनको बसोबास भएको भूभाग मिलाउँदै जाँदा क्रमशः सेनामा पसे। सेवा घरपरिवार र राज्यबीचको सम्बन्धको एउटा रूप थियो, कर र श्रमसँगै।

त्यही सम्बन्धले ब्रिटिश भर्ती-व्यवस्थालाई तयार जमिन भेट्यो। शासकका लागि मानिस दिने र बदलामा जग्गा वा हैसियत पाउने बानी परेको परिवारले रेजिमेन्टका सर्त बुझ्न सक्थ्यो। बदलिएको कुरा मात्रा, नियमितता र पैसा थियो। कम्पनी र पछि क्राउनले नगद तलब र पेन्सन दिए, र पहाडी राज्यले त्यो रूपमा विरलै दिएका थिए।

## [ne] कम्पनीका सर्त र रेजिमेन्टको अर्थतन्त्र

गोर्खाको ब्रिटिश भर्ती सन् १८१४–१६ को आङ्ग्ल–नेपाल युद्धपछि सुरु भयो, जब ईस्ट इन्डिया कम्पनी आफूले लडेका पहाडी सिपाहीबाट प्रभावित भएर तिनलाई भर्ना गर्न थाल्यो। सुरुका सर्त आजको मानकमा उदार थिएनन्, तर नियमित थिए: तलब, खाना, कपडा, केही रूपमा उपचार, र लामो सेवापछि पेन्सनको वाचा। पहाडी घरपरिवारका लागि नियमित नगद आय नयाँ किसिमको स्रोत थियो, र यसले परिवारले आफ्नो जग्गा र श्रमसँग के गर्न सक्छ भन्ने बदल्यो।

अर्थतन्त्र दुई धारिलो थियो। सेवारत सिपाहीको तलब नगदमा आउँथ्यो, जो जग्गा किन्न, ऋण तिर्न, विवाह गर्न वा छोराछोरी पढाउन प्रयोग हुन्थ्यो। पेन्सनले वृद्धावस्थामा सुरक्षा दिन्थ्यो, जो थोरै पहाडी परिवारसँग थियो। तर सेवाले पुरुषलाई वर्षौँ घरबाट टाढा राख्थ्यो, र घरपरिवारले उनको खेती-कामको हिस्सा बोक्नुपर्थ्यो। पति बाहिर हुँदा प्रायः महिलाले खेती, पशु र अधिकारीसँगको काम सँभाल्थे, र परिवारको सफलता सिपाहीको तलबजत्तिकै उनीहरूको श्रममा टेक्थ्यो।

भर्ती डिपो र वर्षैपिच्छे उही जिल्लामा फर्कने अफिसरमार्फत चल्थ्यो; भानसिटार्टको पुस्तिकाले कुन क्षेत्र असल भर्ती-भूमि मानिन्छ, भर्ती कसरी दर्ता हुन्छ र अफिसरले कुन चलनको अपेक्षा गर्ने वर्णन गर्छ, र नर्थी तथा मोरिसले त्यसबाट बनेको रेजिमेन्ट-संसार, सिपाहीका परिवारमाथि राखिएको अपेक्षासमेत देखाउँछन्। रेजिमेन्ट केवल सैन्य इकाइ थिएन; यो आफ्नै कर्मकाण्ड, श्रेणी र सञ्जाल भएको संस्था थियो, र ती सञ्जाल पहाडभित्र पुग्थे।

## [ne] रणनीतिको इकाइ घरपरिवार

आर्थिक इतिहासको सबैभन्दा महत्त्वपूर्ण अन्तर्दृष्टि यो हो: भर्नाको निर्णय प्रायः व्यक्तिको होइन, घरपरिवारको निर्णय थियो। धेरै छोरा भएको परिवारले एउटालाई रेजिमेन्ट पठाएर अरूलाई जग्गामा राख्थ्यो, र तलबले थप जग्गा किन्न वा भाइको पढाइ धान्थ्यो। ऋण भएको परिवारले छोरा पठाएर ऋण मेट्थ्यो। छोरा नभएको परिवारले भतिजा पठाउने व्यवस्था गर्थ्यो। रेजिमेन्टले नगद र हैसियतको बाटो दिन्थ्यो, र घरपरिवारले त्यसलाई कसरी प्रयोग गर्ने हिसाब गर्थ्यो।

यही हिसाबले भर्तीको ढाँचा आकार दियो। राम्रो जग्गा र बलियो घरपरिवार भएका जिल्लाले मानिस पठाउन र तिनको तलबबाट लाभ लिन सक्थे; कम जग्गा भएका जिल्ला सेवामा बढी निर्भर थिए। समयक्रममा सेवा केही क्षेत्रको स्थानीय संस्कृतिको भाग बन्यो, र परिवारको प्रतिष्ठा उसको सैन्य सेवाको अभिलेखसँग जोडिन सक्थ्यो। त्यो संस्कृति ब्रिटिशले आविष्कार गरेको होइन; यो पहाडी घरपरिवार र राज्यबीचको पुरानो सम्बन्धबाट उठ्यो, र सेवाले ल्याएको पेन्सन तथा सुविधाले बलियो बनायो।

मूल्य पनि घरपरिवारभरि बाँडियो। मरेका वा अशक्त भएका सिपाहीले परिवारलाई पेन्सनमा निर्भर छाड्थे, जो दाबी गर्न गाह्रो हुन सक्थ्यो। लामो अनुपस्थितिले विवाहमा तनाव ल्याउन र घरभित्रको अधिकारको सन्तुलन बदल्न सक्थ्यो। र घर फर्किएको पैसा सधैँ खेतीमा खर्च हुँदैनथ्यो: यो व्यापार, घर, छोराको पढाइ वा परिवारको हैसियत जोगाउने कर्मकाण्डी दायित्वमा लगानी हुन सक्थ्यो। रेजिमेन्टको अर्थतन्त्र गाउँको अर्थतन्त्रमा बुनिएको थियो, त्यसबाट अलग थिएन।

## [ne] बीसौँ शताब्दीको परिवर्तन

बीसौँ शताब्दीले दुई विश्वयुद्ध — जसले गुरुङ सिपाहीलाई पहाडबाट टाढाका युद्धमा तान्यो र नयाँ अनुभव, सीप र क्षति ल्यायो — सन् १९५१ मा राणा शासनको अन्त्य, विकासका लागि खुलापन, र विद्यालय, बाटो तथा सरकारी कार्यालयको विस्तार ल्यायो; घान्द्रुकजस्ता गाउँमा पर्यटन, र पछि काठमाडौँ, पोखरा, भारत, बेलायत, हङकङ र अमेरिकातिर बसाइँसराइ आयो। हरेक परिवर्तनले घरेलु अर्थतन्त्र बदल्यो: विद्यालयले नयाँ लगानी र खेतीबाहिरको बाटो बनायो; बाटोले ढुवानीको लागत घटायो र पुराना व्यापार-मार्ग कमजोर बनायो; पर्यटनले लज, गाइड र भरियाको बजार बनायो र पाहुना पुग्ने गाउँमा घर तथा जग्गाको मूल्य बदल्यो; बसाइँसराइले सैन्य तलबलाई प्रतिस्थापन वा पूरक बनाउने रेमिट्यान्स ल्यायो। खेत, बथान, व्यापार र सेवाबीचको पुरानो सन्तुलन क्रमशः बढी विविध र नगद-निर्भर अर्थतन्त्रले प्रतिस्थापन गर्‍यो।

ह्यारिसन र म्याकफर्लेनले जम्मा गरेको दीर्घकालीन अभिलेखले यो परिवर्तन कति असमान थियो देखाउँछ। केही गाउँमा अरूभन्दा दशकौँ अघि बाटो र विद्यालय पुगे। केही परिवार पर्यटन वा पेसामा गए, अरू खेती र रेमिट्यान्समा निर्भर रहे। पुरानो अर्थतन्त्रमा भएको असमानता — पशु र जग्गा भएका र नभएका परिवारबीच — हराएन; यो नयाँ स्रोतवरिपरि पुनर्गठित भयो। त्यसैले गुरुङ अर्थतन्त्रको इतिहास परम्पराबाट आधुनिकतामा संक्रमणको कथा होइन, एउटै घरेलु समस्याको बारम्बार पुनर्गठनको कथा हो: अनिश्चित भूदृश्यबाट जीविका कसरी सुरक्षित गर्ने।

## [ne] अर्थतन्त्रले गुरुङ इतिहासबारे के व्याख्या गर्छ

यसले पुरानो आर्थिक व्यवस्था किन जोखिमपूर्ण थियो पनि व्याख्या गर्छ। ऋतुअनुसारको चरन, चलनको अधिकार र व्यापार-मार्गमा निर्भर घरपरिवारलाई बाटो, सीमा बन्द, जग्गा-कानुनको परिवर्तन वा नयाँ बजारले अवरुद्ध गर्न सक्थ्यो। बीसौँ शताब्दीले यी सबै ल्यायो, र नतिजा पुरानो अर्थतन्त्रको अन्त्य होइन, राज्य, सेना, पर्यटन र रेमिट्यान्सकेन्द्रित नगद अर्थतन्त्रअन्तर्गत त्यसको क्रमिक अधीनता बन्यो।

आर्थिक इतिहासले केवल सैन्य वा सांस्कृतिक विवरणले व्याख्या गर्न नसक्ने धेरै कुरा व्याख्या गर्छ। यसले सैन्य सेवा किन अतार्किक नभई आकर्षक थियो पनि व्याख्या गर्छ — पहाडी अर्थतन्त्रमा नियमित तलब र पेन्सन साँच्चै दुर्लभ वस्तु थिए — र सेवा व्यक्तिको साहस होइन, घरपरिवारको रणनीति किन थियो, महिलाको श्रम किन त्यसको केन्द्रमा थियो, र रेजिमेन्ट गुरुङ पहाडको सबैभन्दा महत्त्वपूर्ण संस्थामध्ये किन बन्यो।

अभिलेखका लागि पाठ यो हो: गुरुङ इतिहास राजा र अभियानको क्रम भनेर लेख्न सकिँदैन। गहिरो अतीत ठाडो भूदृश्यमा काम गर्ने, राज्यसँग मोलमोलाइ गर्ने, पशु र वस्तु सार्ने, र सर्त बदलिँदा अनुकूलन गर्ने घरपरिवारको इतिहास हो। रेजिमेन्ट त्यही इतिहासको भाग हो, त्यसमाथि टाँसिएको छुट्टै अध्याय होइन। र घरपरिवार — यसका खेत, पशु, दायित्व र महिलाको श्रमसहित — नै त्यो इकाइ हो जसले पूरै कथा बुझ्नयोग्य बनाउँछ।
