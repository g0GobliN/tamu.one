## [en] A landscape of small states

For several centuries the hills of central Nepal were divided into dozens of small kingdoms, often grouped as the Chaubisi ("twenty-four") states. The lists vary, but they commonly include **Kaski, Lamjung, Tanahun, Gorkha, Parbat, Bhirkot, Nuwakot, Garahun, Rising, Ghiring, Gulmi, Argha, Khanchi, Dhurkot, Musikot, Isma and Pyuthan**, with others named in some accounts and omitted in others. Kaski and Lamjung, which include much of the Gurung homeland around today's Pokhara, Ghandruk, Sikles and Besisahar, were among the most important, and their courts are the ones most often remembered in Gurung traditions. The number twenty-four was itself a convention rather than a census: the same term could cover a shifting set of allies, rivals and clients.

## [ne] साना राज्यको भूदृश्य

केही शताब्दीसम्म मध्य नेपालका पहाड दर्जनौँ साना राज्यमा बाँडिएका थिए, प्रायः चौबीसी ("चौबीस") राज्यका रूपमा समूहीकृत। सूची फरक पर्छ, तर सामान्यतः **कास्की, लमजुङ, तनहुँ, गोरखा, पर्वत, भिर्कोट, नुवाकोट, गरहुँ, रिसिङ, घिरिङ, गुल्मी, अर्घा, खाँची, धुर्कोट, मुसिकोट, इस्मा र प्युठान** पर्छन्; कुनै विवरणमा अरू नाम थपिन्छन्, कुनैमा हटाइन्छन्। आजको पोखरा, घान्द्रुक, सिक्लेस र बेसीशहर वरपर गुरुङ मातृभूमिको ठूलो भाग पर्ने कास्की र लमजुङ तिनैमा सबैभन्दा महत्त्वपूर्ण थिए, र गुरुङ परम्परामा सबैभन्दा धेरै सम्झिने तिनैका दरबार हुन्। चौबीस भन्ने सङ्ख्या आफैँ जनगणना होइन, अभ्यास थियो: एउटै शब्दले बदलिँदो सहयोगी, प्रतिद्वन्द्वी र अधीनस्थ राज्यको समूह जनाउन सक्थ्यो।

## [en] Ghale memories of rule

Many Gurung traditions remember Ghale chiefs as the earlier rulers of the region, before newer ruling lineages took power. Mani Raj Gurung’s historical study discusses how power, knowledge and identity shifted between these groups. Exact dates and events are uncertain and vary by village story.

## [ne] घले शासनको सम्झना

धेरै गुरुङ परम्पराले नयाँ शासक वंशले शक्ति लिनुअघि घले मुखियालाई क्षेत्रको प्रारम्भिक शासकका रूपमा सम्झन्छन्। Mani Raj Gurung को ऐतिहासिक अध्ययनले यी समूहबीच शक्ति, ज्ञान र पहिचान कसरी सर्यो चर्चा गर्छ। ठ्याक्कै मिति र घटना अनिश्चित छन् र गाउँको कथाअनुसार फरक पर्छन्।

## [en] Village life under the kingdoms

Gurung villages kept much of their own way of life: local headmen, clan customs, priests such as pachyu and klyepri, and seasonal farming and herding. Ties to the royal court came mainly through tribute, labour duties and local officials.

## [ne] राज्यमुनिको गाउँ जीवन

गुरुङ गाउँले आफ्नो धेरै जीवनशैली कायम राखे: स्थानीय मुखिया, क्लेका चलन, पाच्यु र क्ल्येप्रीजस्ता पुरोहित, र मौसमी खेती तथा गोठालोपन। दरबारसँगको साइनो मुख्यतः भेटी, श्रम-दायित्व र स्थानीय अधिकारीमार्फत हुन्थ्यो।

## [en] Lamjung and the rise of Gorkha

The Shah dynasty that later unified Nepal traced its origin to Lamjung before establishing itself in Gorkha. This makes the Gurung heartland part of the background story of the modern Nepali state, even though historians caution against reading later national history back into village life.

## [ne] लमजुङ र गोरखाको उदय

पछि नेपाल एकीकरण गर्ने शाह वंशले गोरखामा स्थापित हुनुअघि आफ्नो उत्पत्ति लमजुङमा देखाउँछ। यसले गुरुङ मातृभूमिलाई आधुनिक नेपाली राज्यको पृष्ठभूमि-कथाको हिस्सा बनाउँछ, यद्यपि इतिहासकारले पछिको राष्ट्रिय इतिहासलाई गाउँको जीवनमा उल्टो पढ्नबाट सचेत गराउँछन्।

## [en] Tax, labour and the mints

The hill kingdoms that bordered the Gurung areas were small but not simple. They raised revenue through land tax, levies on trade, fines and labour service; they minted coins, granted land to temples and officials, and recorded obligations in documents that sometimes survive. For a Gurung household, the state appeared as a demand: a share of the harvest, days of work on a road or a fort, a soldier for a campaign. It also appeared as a court, a source of legitimacy and a market for surplus. The relationship was negotiated locally, through headmen and clan leaders who mediated between the village and the palace, and that mediation is one of the ways in which Gurung political institutions developed.

## [ne] कर, श्रम र टकसार

गुरुङ क्षेत्रसँग जोडिएका पहाडी राज्य साना थिए, तर सरल थिएनन्। तिनले जग्गाको कर, व्यापारमा लेवी, जरिवाना र श्रम-सेवाबाट आय उठाउँथे; सिक्का चलाउँथे, मन्दिर र अधिकारीलाई जग्गा दान गर्थे, र कहिलेकाहीँ बाँच्ने दस्तावेजमा दायित्व रेकर्ड गर्थे। गुरुङ घरका लागि राज्य मागका रूपमा देखिन्थ्यो: बालीको अंश, सडक वा किल्लामा केही दिनको काम, अभियानका लागि एक सिपाही। यो अदालत, वैधताको स्रोत र बढी उत्पादनको बजारका रूपमा पनि देखिन्थ्यो। सम्बन्ध स्थानीय रूपमा मोलमोलाई हुन्थ्यो, गाउँ र दरबारबीच मध्यस्थता गर्ने मुखिया र क्ले-नेतामार्फत, र त्यही मध्यस्थता गुरुङ राजनीतिक संस्था विकसित हुने बाटोमध्ये एक हो।

## [en] What the kingdoms left behind

The kingdoms disappeared into the Gorkha state, but their administrative habits survived. Land tenure categories, revenue officials, festival patronage and the mapping of villages onto districts all carry traces of the older order. So do the genealogies and titles that families kept, and the local shrines that were patronised by rulers and are still maintained by their descendants’ villages. Historians of Nepal have increasingly turned to these local records, and the Gurung areas are part of that turn: the story is no longer only the story of Kathmandu and Gorkha, but of dozens of small polities whose decisions shaped the lives of the people who later became Nepali citizens.

## [ne] राज्यहरूले छोडेका कुरा

राज्यहरू गोर्खा राज्यमा हराए, तर तिनका प्रशासनिक बानी बाँचे। जग्गा-स्वामित्वका कोटि, राजस्व अधिकारी, चाडको संरक्षण र गाउँलाई जिल्लामा नक्साङ्कन गर्ने तरिकाले पुरानो व्यवस्थाका चिन्ह बोक्छन्। परिवारले राखेका वंशावली र उपाधि, र शासकले संरक्षण गरेका र अझै तिनका उत्तराधिकारीका गाउँले राख्ने स्थानीय देवालय पनि त्यसैका चिन्ह हुन्। नेपालका इतिहासकार यी स्थानीय अभिलेखतिर बढ्दो रूपमा फर्केका छन्, र गुरुङ क्षेत्र त्यसैको अंश हो: कथा अब काठमाडौँ र गोर्खाको मात्र होइन, पछि नेपाली नागरिक बनेका मानिसको जीवन घडाउने दर्जनौँ साना राज्यको पनि हो।

## [en] The twenty-four, the twenty-two and the list that does not add up

Nepali historiography usually divides the hill states west of Kathmandu into the baise, twenty-two, and the chaubisi, twenty-four, and the two numbers have been repeated so often that they look like a roll call. They are not. The lists differ from chronicle to chronicle, some states appear in one account and not in another, and the counts describe a later classificatory habit rather than a league that ever met. The sources used in this library name Kaski, Lamjung, Tanahun, Gorkha, Parbat, Bhirkot, Nuwakot, Garahun, Rising, Ghiring, Gulmi, Argha, Khanchi, Dhurkot, Musikot, Isma and Pyuthan — seventeen states — and they do not supply a single authoritative list of the remaining seven. Rather than invent names to fill the number, the library prints the seventeen it can document and says plainly that the count is a convention: what mattered locally was not membership of a league of twenty-four but the relations between neighbours — marriage alliances, tribute, disputed pasture, and the obligation to send soldiers when a raja called.

For Gurung households those relations were concrete. A village on the edge of two states could pay tax to one, graze in the other and marry across the boundary; a local chief could switch allegiance when the balance of power changed. The chaubisi and baise are useful as a map of that political landscape, not as a census of it, and the smaller thapas and kots that the chronicles omit were often the ones a Gurung family actually dealt with.
