## [en] A language written three ways

Tamu Kyi has been written in Devanagari, in Roman letters and, since the 1990s, in the Gurung Khema script. Each choice connects the language to a different public. Devanagari is what children learn first in Nepal, so it is the practical script for schoolbooks, song sheets and community notices. Roman letters dominate linguistic work, dictionaries and online chat, usually with the tone marks left out. Khema is the community's own invention, taught in classes and used on signs and in ritual texts, and since 2024 it has been part of Unicode. The three coexist, and most literate Gurung readers can move between at least two of them.

## [ne] तीन तरिकाले लेखिएको भाषा

## [en] Missionaries, schools and the first grammars

The first systematic writing of Gurung was done by missionaries and colonial scholars in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. They needed alphabets for hymns, catechisms and dictionaries, and they produced orthographies that varied from mission to mission and often ignored tone. Nepali-language schooling, expanding from the late nineteenth century, gave Gurung children literacy in Devanagari but not in their own language. The result was a familiar colonial pattern: a spoken language documented by outsiders for religious and administrative purposes, while its speakers were educated in another language entirely.

## [ne] मिसनरी, विद्यालय र पहिलो व्याकरण

गुरुङको पहिलो व्यवस्थित लेखन उन्नाइसौँ र बीसौँ शताब्दीका सुरुमा मिसनरी र औपनिवेशिक विद्वानले गरे। उनीहरूलाई भजन, धर्मशिक्षा र शब्दकोशका लागि वर्णमाला चाहिन्थ्यो, र तिनले मिसनअनुसार फरक पर्ने र प्रायः सुर बेवास्ता गर्ने हिज्जे बनाए। उन्नाइसौँ शताब्दीको अन्त्यदेखि फैलिएको नेपाली-माध्यम विद्यालयले गुरुङ बालबालिकालाई देवनागरीमा साक्षर बनायो, तर आफ्नै भाषामा होइन। नतिजा परिचित औपनिवेशिक ढाँचा बन्यो: धार्मिक र प्रशासनिक उद्देश्यका लागि बाहिरीले अभिलेख गरेको बोलिने भाषा, जबकि त्यसका वक्ता पूरै अर्को भाषामा शिक्षित हुँदै।

## [en] The invention of Khema

The Gurung Khema script was developed in the 1990s by community activists who wanted a writing system that belonged to the language rather than borrowed from Nepali or Tibetan. It is a syllabic script with its own letter shapes, vowel signs and numerals, and it was promoted through classes, primers and community organisations. Inventing a script is a political act as much as a technical one: it asserts that the language is capable of literacy on its own terms, gives the community a visible marker of identity, and creates a new set of things to teach, print and standardise. It also creates a new division, between those who read Khema and those who do not.

## [ne] खेमाको आविष्कार

गुरुङ खेमा लिपि १९९० को दशकमा ती सामुदायिक कार्यकर्ताले विकसित गरे जो नेपाली वा तिब्बतीबाट सापटी नलिइएको, भाषाकै आफ्नै लेखन-प्रणाली चाहन्थे। यो आफ्नै अक्षर-आकृति, स्वर-चिन्ह र अंक भएको अक्षरात्मक लिपि हो, र कक्षा, प्राइमर तथा सामुदायिक संस्थामार्फत यसको प्रचार भयो। लिपि आविष्कार गर्नु प्राविधिकजत्तिकै राजनीतिक काम हो: यसले भाषा आफ्नै सर्तमा साक्षरताको योग्य छ भन्ने दाबी गर्छ, समुदायलाई देखिने पहिचान-चिन्ह दिन्छ, र सिकाउन, छाप्न तथा मानक बनाउन नयाँ कुराको समूह सिर्जना गर्छ। यसले नयाँ विभाजन पनि बनाउँछ — खेमा पढ्ने र नपढ्नेबीच।

## [en] Unicode 2024

In September 2024 the Unicode Consortium published version 16.0, which for the first time included the Gurung Khema script as a encoded block. The practical effect is that the script can be typed, stored and displayed reliably on ordinary computers and phones without special fonts or workarounds — a condition most literate communities take for granted and most minority scripts do not have. Encoding is not revitalisation: a script can be available on every device and still not be used. But it removes a real barrier, and it gives schools, publishers and software developers a stable standard to build on.

## [en] Suppression and revival

The story of writing Tamu Kyi is also a story of what was not allowed. Under the Panchayat system (1960–1990), Nepal promoted the Nepali language and Hindu cultural forms as national unity, and minority languages had little place in schools or broadcasting; teaching material in Gurung was scarce and community organisations were restricted. The restoration of multi-party democracy in 1990 opened space for ethnic organisations, language classes and script activism, and the 2002 recognition of the Gurung as an indigenous nationality gave the movement institutional footing. The 2015 constitution's language provisions are broader on paper than in practice, and the gap between the right and the classroom is where most of the work now happens.
